# 9. Animation Board

The "**Animation Board**" is for animations of your own, made once and used again and again: drawings, logos that trace themselves out, characters that walk and run, 3D text. A finished board can go on a video, be saved as one of your elements, or go into a packaging style.

Get there with "**Animation Board**" in the bottom row of the home screen. When you open a board, AI Onset flies over and settles beside it, so you can ask it for help as you work.

## 9-1 A new board

Click "New board" and choose the shape (9:16, 16:9, 1:1) and length (up to 20 seconds). The background can be a colour, or transparent (to lay it over a video).

Under the picture are the **layer tracks**, one per layer. Drag a layer left or right to move it in time, and pull its edge to make it longer or shorter. Layers on the same track that overlap in time each get a row of their own.

## 9-2 Things you can draw

- **Six brushes**: Pen, Marker, Crayon, Pencil, Watercolour and Ink, with pressure sensitivity. When it plays, each stroke "draws itself" in the direction you drew it.
- **Pen tool**: click out anchors to draw a shape; you can drag the anchors afterwards.
- **Logo (SVG)**: bring in an SVG file and each shape becomes a layer, traced out one after another, so the logo draws itself.
- **Pictures**: your own or from the AI library. Use one as a layer that moves, or dim and lock it to use it only as a **tracing guide** (it doesn't show in the finished animation).
- **3D**: shapes, logos and text can be pushed out into 3D (Metal, Glass, Clay, Matte), and you can also add a 3D model (.glb).
- **Pattern**: one layer repeated as a grid, brick, ring or scattered, which can scroll or grow and shrink.
- **Frame by frame**: like a flipbook, drawn one frame at a time, with a faint copy of the previous frame to guide you. Set the frames per second, and whether it loops or stops on the last frame.
- **Shadow**: each layer can have a shadow (Soft, Hard, Long, Floating, Glow); drag the little sun to change where the light comes from.

## 9-3 Have the AI draw a character

Tell AI Onset beside the board what you want, for example "Draw an office worker in a suit rushing to work with a briefcase, flat illustration style".

- The AI first plans the pose, colours and parts, draws several parts at the same time, checks the whole drawing once it's done, and redraws any parts that didn't come out well.
- The finished character is **split into parts** (head, eyes, mouth, arms, legs…), one layer per part, with the joints already set up: the eyes blink and the mouth talks.
- For a particular drawing style, give it reference pictures (drag them into the chat). That works better than describing it in words. It only learns the drawing style from them; it doesn't copy the characters.
- If it stopped halfway (or the app closed), say "**go on**" and it only draws the parts not drawn yet.

### Draw a character that looks like you

Give it a photo of yourself and it draws a cartoon that's recognisably you: face shape, hair, glasses, skin tone, clothes and build all follow the photo, and once it's drawn it checks it against the photo twice.

- Pick the photo from the AI library, choose a file yourself, or drag it into the chat.
- Or just say "use me at 12 seconds in 'some video'", and it takes that frame from the video itself.
- When it's done it also draws closed eyes and three mouths (closed, half open, open), so as soon as it's on a video it blinks and talks along with the voice-over.
- The photo stays on this Mac and only goes to the AI you already use, to do the drawing.

## 9-4 Make the character move

**Skeleton actions**: tell AI Onset "run from 0–8 seconds, and look at the watch while running at 3–5 seconds", and the character's legs really run and the arm really lifts. There are over 50 actions:

- Moving: walk, brisk walk, run, sprint, tiptoe, jump, climb stairs…
- Everyday: look at a watch, talk on the phone, drink, type, sit down, stretch…
- Feelings: wave, nod, shake the head, shrug, cheer, face-palm, thumbs up, finger heart…
- Talking: talking gestures, open-hands explaining, counting on fingers, pointing at yourself, pointing at the viewer…

Actions can be layered on top of each other. Feet stay planted on the ground without sliding. Animals have skeletons of their own: four-legged animals (walk, trot, gallop, wag the tail, bark), birds (take off, flap, glide), fish (swim, dart, turn) and hoppers like rabbits.

Also:

- **Soft joint**: knees and elbows bend in one smooth curve instead of two stiff pieces. Good for cute, soft drawing styles, animals, sleeves and trouser legs.
- **Swap pictures**: other looks for the same part, such as closed eyes, an open mouth, a fist, or the head turned three-quarters. The AI can draw them for you from the original.
- **Lip-sync**: when the mouth has three pictures (closed, half open, open), it opens and closes along with the sound once it's on a video. You can also tell AI Onset "make him talk along with voice-over line 3": the mouth then only follows that part of the voice-over, and the character is placed where that line starts by itself.
- **Puppet**: no character yet? Start with a basic puppet, then swap its parts for your own pictures.

## 9-5 Camera

A board starts as a fixed frame, but you can add camera moves: push in, pull out, pan, tilt up and down, a slight roll; the camera can **follow a character**; it can **shake** on a landing; far and near layers get **depth parallax**; and captions or a logo can stay fixed on the screen without moving with the camera.

## 9-6 Organising, saving, versions

- **Compound layers**: hold ⌘ or Shift and click a few layers, then click "**Merge into a compound layer**". The whole group moves, turns and takes keyframes together, and each layer's own animation inside it is kept. "Take apart into its layers" splits them up again.
- **Autosave**: it saves itself after every change.
- **Versions**: a version is kept before the AI changes anything and whenever you click "**Keep a version**". "**Go back to a version**" at the top takes you back, and how it is now is kept first.

## 9-7 Put it on a video

- In Editing, click "**Add a board…**" at the top and pick one. It goes at the playhead, with a transparent background, over the video.
- If you change the board and add it again, the one already there is replaced with the new look.
- You can also "**Save as an element**" (for use in flying text) or "**Use in a packaging style**" as an intro or name strip (chapter 8).

### On the iPhone

The phone can open boards too. Scroll the tracks with your finger; to move a layer, **hold it for about 0.3 seconds** (it buzzes), then drag.
